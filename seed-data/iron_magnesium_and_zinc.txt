Iron, Magnesium and Zinc
Daily needs, deficiency signs and absorption tips for three minerals that commonly run low in athletes, with special attention to iron in female athletes.

Iron requirements (RDA)
- Men 19 and older: 8 mg per day.
- Women 19-50: 18 mg per day; women 51 and older: 8 mg per day.
- Teens 14-18: boys 11 mg, girls 15 mg. Pregnancy: 27 mg.
- Vegetarians need about 1.8 times more iron because plant iron is less bioavailable.
- Upper limit for adults: 45 mg per day. Supplements at 45 mg or more can cause nausea and constipation.

Heme vs non-heme iron
- Heme iron comes from meat, poultry and seafood and is well absorbed; non-heme iron comes from plants, legumes and fortified grains.
- Iron absorption is about 14-18% from mixed diets with meat and vitamin C, and 5-12% from vegetarian diets.
- Enhancers of non-heme iron: vitamin C (citrus, peppers, berries) and meat, fish or poultry in the same meal.
- Inhibitors: phytate in grains and beans, polyphenols in tea, coffee and some legumes, and calcium, which can reduce both heme and non-heme absorption. Take iron and calcium supplements at different times of day.
- Spinach contains iron but absorbs poorly because of polyphenols.

Iron status and ferritin
- Serum ferritin reflects iron stores and drops first in iron depletion, before anemia appears.
- General clinical cutoffs: ferritin below 30 mcg/L suggests iron deficiency; below 10 mcg/L suggests iron deficiency anemia.
- Athlete staging: non-anemic iron deficiency stage 1 is ferritin under 30 mcg/L with transferrin saturation above 16%; stage 2 is ferritin under 20 mcg/L with saturation under 16%, while hemoglobin is still at least 120 g/L. Some sports research uses ferritin under 40 mcg/L as the cutoff.
- Inflammation can falsely raise ferritin, so tests are best done at rest, not right after hard training.

Iron in female athletes
- Up to 60% of female athletes experience iron deficiency, due to menstrual losses, high training loads and sometimes low energy intake.
- Hard exercise raises the hormone hepcidin, peaking about 3 hours after training, which temporarily blocks iron absorption. Low energy availability and low-carbohydrate diets increase this response.
- Iron was absorbed better after morning training than after afternoon training, so morning post-exercise meals may be a good time for iron-rich foods.
- Symptoms of deficiency: fatigue, weakness, poor concentration, reduced endurance and feeling cold. Iron supplements should be guided by a blood test and a doctor or sports dietitian.

Magnesium requirements (RDA)
- Men 19-30: 400 mg per day; men 31 and older: 420 mg per day.
- Women 19-30: 310 mg per day; women 31 and older: 320 mg per day.
- Upper limit applies to supplements only (not food): 350 mg per day for adults. Above this, diarrhea, nausea and abdominal cramps are common.

Magnesium deficiency and sources
- Early signs: loss of appetite, nausea, fatigue and weakness. Later: numbness, tingling, muscle cramps and contractions, abnormal heart rhythm.
- Food sources: pumpkin seeds 28 g = 156 mg; chia seeds 28 g = 111 mg; almonds 28 g = 80 mg; boiled spinach half cup = 78 mg; black beans half cup = 60 mg.
- Supplement forms: aspartate, citrate, lactate and chloride are absorbed better than magnesium oxide and sulfate.

Zinc requirements (RDA)
- Men 19 and older: 11 mg per day. Women 19 and older: 8 mg per day.
- Pregnancy: 11 mg; breastfeeding: 12 mg.
- Upper limit for adults: 40 mg per day from food and supplements.
- Vegetarians may need up to 50% more zinc because phytates in legumes and whole grains bind it.

Zinc deficiency and sources
- Signs: impaired immune function and frequent infections, loss of appetite, altered taste and smell, slow wound healing, hair loss and diarrhea.
- Zinc absorption from food ranges from 5% to over 50% depending on phytate intake. Soaking beans, grains and seeds before cooking, sprouting, fermenting and leavened bread reduce phytate.
- Sources: oysters (about 28-32 mg per 85 g), beef sirloin 3.8 mg per 85 g, pumpkin seeds 2.2 mg per 28 g, pork chop 1.9 mg per 85 g.

Zinc-copper interaction
- Taking 50 mg or more of zinc per day for weeks can block copper absorption, lower immune function and reduce HDL cholesterol. Long-term excess can cause copper deficiency with anemia and nerve damage.
- Very high zinc doses (142 mg per day) also interfere with magnesium absorption.
