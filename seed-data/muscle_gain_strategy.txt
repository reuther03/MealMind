Muscle Gain Strategy
How big a calorie surplus to use when building muscle, how fast beginners and advanced lifters should gain weight, lean bulk versus dirty bulk, body recomposition, and how to track progress during a bulk.

Why a surplus helps
A positive energy balance is anabolic on its own, and combining it with resistance training directs that extra energy toward building skeletal muscle. The goal of a gaining phase (also called a bulk or off-season) is to add as much muscle as possible while limiting fat gain. Because the rate of muscle growth is limited, eating far more than needed mostly adds fat.

Size of the calorie surplus
- A surplus of about 10-20% above maintenance calories is recommended for natural lifters.
- Bodybuilders eat on average about 45 kcal per kg of body weight in the off-season, so the recommended surplus works out to roughly 42-48 kcal/kg per day; the percentage above individual maintenance is what matters.
- In elite athletes training 4 days per week for 8-12 weeks, a group eating about 600 kcal per day more than an ad libitum group gained slightly more lean mass (1.7 kg versus 1.2 kg, not statistically different) but much more fat (1.1 kg versus 0.2 kg). The researchers concluded that a surplus of 200-300 kcal per day is more appropriate than 500 kcal for highly trained people.
- Untrained people are further from their genetic ceiling; in one study a very large surplus of about 2000 kcal combined with lifting produced weight gain that was almost entirely lean mass.

Recommended rate of weight gain
- Beginners and intermediates: about 0.25-0.5% of body weight per week, which is roughly 1-2% of body weight per month.
- Advanced lifters: use a smaller surplus and aim for the lower end of this range or slower. For an advanced lifter, gaining 2 kg per month is likely too fast and adds unnecessary fat.
- In absolute terms, about 0.25-0.5 kg per week is a common target for gaining lean mass while limiting fat.
- Immediately after a hard diet or competition, a faster regain for a short time can help restore health, then the rate should be slowed.

Lean bulk versus dirty bulk
A lean bulk uses a modest surplus and controlled food choices, accepting slower scale progress to keep fat gain low. A dirty bulk means eating large amounts of any food to gain weight quickly. Because extra calories beyond what muscle can use are stored as fat, a dirty bulk mostly lengthens the next fat-loss phase. More body fat means a longer or harsher cut later, with more time spent in low energy availability. The lean bulk is the evidence-based default, especially for trained lifters.

Body recomposition
Recomposition means losing fat and gaining muscle at the same time, at roughly stable body weight. It is most realistic for beginners, people returning after a break, and people with higher body fat, because their muscle-building potential is high and their stored energy is large. Studies of dieting athletes who lost weight slowly have also shown small gains in lean mass. For lean, experienced lifters, recomposition is slow, and separate gaining and cutting phases usually work better. Recomp still requires progressive resistance training and high protein intake.

Macronutrient priorities during a bulk
Set protein first, then a moderate amount of fat, and give the remaining calories to carbohydrate to fuel training volume. The specific gram targets for each macronutrient are covered in the protein, fat and carbohydrate guides.

Tracking progress
- Weigh yourself daily or several times per week under the same conditions (morning, after the toilet, before eating) and use the weekly average to remove day-to-day water fluctuations.
- Compare the weekly average with the target rate; if gain is too fast, trim calories slightly; if too slow, add a little.
- Track waist circumference; if the waist grows much faster than other measurements, the surplus is probably too large.
- Track strength in key lifts and take regular progress photos in the same lighting.
- Reassess body composition every few months if possible.

How long to bulk
Bulking phases commonly last several months. End the phase or switch to maintenance if body fat rises to a level that would make the next diet long and difficult.
